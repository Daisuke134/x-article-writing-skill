# X Article writing

Write a long-form X Article that a builder audience reads to the end, bookmarks, and replies to. Every rule here comes from a top-performing article or an open-source writing skill listed in Sources.

## Before you write

1. Read 3 to 5 current winners in your niche. Search X for `url:"x.com/i/article"` plus your topic, last 60 days. Record views, likes, bookmarks, replies, and word count. Copy the shape of the best one.
2. Collect your real numbers with their source and timestamp. If a number is unmeasured, write "unmeasured". Never estimate a result and present it as measured.
3. Write the one sentence the reader should remember. If you can't, don't start.

## Title

- One concrete claim with a real number or a time box, in first person. 8 to 16 words.
- Patterns that win: "N days on X: A to B", "I lost $X (the real story)", "Stop doing X for every Y", "What actually worked in X this week".
- Put the tension in the title: a number, then a twist ("I cut the waste. Then the work stopped.").

## Opening

- Line 1 is the hard fact. No context before it.
- Line 2 is the twist.
- Line 3 is the map: what happened, what worked, what didn't, what's next.
- State limits up front: how you counted, what you could not measure.

## Body

- Paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences. One idea per paragraph.
- A sentence-case heading every 3 to 5 paragraphs. Headings carry the point.
- Order: what happened with dates, the numbers, what you changed, what you copied (name and link the person), what went wrong, what you'd do if you started over, what's next.
- One visual block that reads in 3 seconds: a short table or a code-block diagram under 40 characters wide.
- Lists only for numbers, routines, or steps.
- Someone else's number stays theirs. Write "X reported Y on their own test. That is their number."
- Include the failure. It is usually the most quoted part.

## Close

- 3 concrete next steps.
- One specific question that invites replies.
- One follow CTA and one link to your proof (dashboard, repo, or data).

## Teaser post

- Line 1: the hard number.
- Line 2: what you did, and the twist.
- Line 3: "I wrote down what worked and what didn't."
- Under 270 characters. No hashtags. Let the article card carry the link.

## Cover image

One big real number on a plain background with a one-line caption. No stock robots or generic AI art.

## Humanizer pass (required)

Run this before you publish. Rewrite any line that fails.

- No "not X, it's Y" or "not just X, but Y".
- No stacked groups of three. Use the natural count.
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use a period or a comma.
- No AI vocabulary: delve, crucial, pivotal, landscape, leverage, robust, seamless, showcase, testament, underscore, foster, elevate, streamline.
- No "serves as" or "stands as". Write "is".
- No vague attributions. Name the source or cut the claim.
- No generic conclusions. End on a fact or a plan.
- No forced rhythm ("Short. Punchy. Done."). Mix sentence lengths where the content calls for it.
- Straight quotes. Sentence-case headings. No emoji in headings.

## Final check

- [ ] Every number has a source and a timestamp you can show.
- [ ] Unmeasured items say "unmeasured".
- [ ] Every copied idea names and links its author.
- [ ] Title has a number and a twist.
- [ ] First line is the hard fact.
- [ ] 900 to 1,300 words for a story, up to about 2,900 for a breakdown.
- [ ] One question for replies, one follow CTA.
- [ ] Teaser under 270 characters.
- [ ] Humanizer pass done.
- [ ] Published language matches the audience (English for a global audience).
